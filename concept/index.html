The game

You're a blob. The world is sand, water, fire and ice. Everything is a puzzle piece, especially you.

Pixelbob is a platformer where every pixel is simulated: rock holds, sand pours, water finds its level, fire spreads. Bob is made of the same stuff, a few hundred particles of goo that you steer by squishing. Warm him up and he runs like honey. Chill him and he sets like a brick. Every room comes down to the same question: what shape, what temperature, and where?

You are the tool

No keys in your pocket, no levers to carry. You pour, plug, dangle, weigh and freeze. Bob is a Swiss army knife made of jelly.

The world is real

Every pixel is stuff. If it looks like it should work, it does. If it looks like it should hurt, it really does.

Heat is your wardrobe

Runny to drain through a crack. Frozen to bridge a lake. Normal to, well, roll about. Dressing for the room is half the puzzle.

You are the score

Spikes, lava and big drops cost you bits of Bob. Every level asks one question: how much of you made it home?

Hard to kill, easy to chip

Bob sags off ledges, clings on, oozes back. Sloppy play survives. Good play arrives in one piece.

Bob

Bob is goo: about 280 particles, one per pixel, held together by surface tension and a stubborn wish to be round. He wobbles when he lands, leans into a roll, and sags when he can't be bothered to hold his shape. Nothing about him is animated by hand. Every Bob on this page is the real one, simulated into his pose.

What he can do

Roll
A tank tread made of jelly. Momentum is your friend, right up to the edge.
Jump
Squash down to charge, let go to boing.
Pour
Runny, he drains into cracks and under doors like spilt syrup.
Dangle
Hang off a ledge on a string of yourself. Too far, and you drip.
Climb
Stick to tacky walls. Push in to hold on, up to climb.
Split
Leave a bit of yourself on a switch. Come back for it. Probably.
Merge
Bits that touch become Bob again, no questions asked.
Absorb
Drink water and get heavy. Drink glow goo and become a lamp.

Heat only comes from things you can see: fires, lava, vents, snow, ice. Bob warms up on the side facing the fire, holds the heat for a while, then lets it go. Hot Bob melts snow. Frozen Bob freezes any water he touches, which is either a bridge or a very cold mistake.

Look and feel

Crisp pixels, real light, deep shadow. Most of every screen is dark, and the light tells you where to look: a fire, a pool of glowing goo, a lost bit of Bob glinting on a ledge.

Every pixel counts

One pixel on screen is one cell of the simulation, on a 320×180 camera. Bob is 20 pixels across: small enough that the world feels big.

Light is real

Lamps cast soft shadows, sunlight seeps into rock, and anything that glows lights up the room around it.

Warm against cool

Every world has two lights fighting over the screen. The warm one is where the heat is: the danger, and usually the way out.

Colours are a language

The ground is dark and quiet. Bright colour is saved for Bob, for danger and for anything you can use, so a player who knows the colours can read a room before setting foot in it.

Bob's colour

Worlds

Bob goes down. Seven worlds, from the meadows on the surface to the molten core, each with its own stuff and its own light. Each one teaches a new trick, then makes you use it with all the others.

The map

The map is a slice through the earth. Every world is a layer deeper than the last.

Menus and HUD

The game never leaves the screen. Menus are dark glass and a pixel font, and they get out of the way the moment you're done with them.
