# 11 Worlds

How the game is laid out: a run of worlds, each a biome with its own materials and its own light, and each teaching Bob one new thing. This is a proposal to play against, not a script. The shots in `prototypes/00-stills` (one per world) show what each one feels like.

## Principles

- **One new verb per world.** Each world introduces one thing Bob can do or one thing that's done to him, then spends its levels combining it with everything before. By the last world, every puzzle has three or four answers.
- **The material set is the teacher.** A world's new materials *are* its mechanic: moss teaches grip, glow goo teaches light, ice teaches cold. A player who has learned the colours can read a room before touching it.
- **Two-colour light story per world.** Each world has a warm and a cool source that fight over the screen (see [02](02-art-direction.md#palette)). The warm one is usually where the heat (and the danger, and the help) comes from.
- **Mass is the score everywhere.** Every world has lost bits to find and ways to lose them. A clean run brings more of Bob home; a messy one still finishes.
- **Heat only from what you can see.** Each world has its own heat sources and sinks, so temperature puzzles change character from world to world.
- **Order is soft.** The early worlds are fixed (they teach); later ones could be chosen from a map.

## The run

## 1. The Green

Rolling hills, ponds and trees: the tutorial world, and the one people see first, so it has to be beautiful in a calm way.

- **Teaches:** rolling and coasting, charged jumps, squashing down, momentum over hills, that water is a trap you can climb out of, that bits of you can be lost and found, and that **green moss grips** (he can roll up a moss slope too steep for rock).
- **Puzzles:** build speed downhill to crest a rise; jump across ponds; find the lost bit at the top of a moss ramp; don't fall in the pond with the bit in it.
- **Shot:** golden hour. Bob rolls down a hill towards a moss ramp that climbs to a ledge with a lost bit glinting on it. Trees, a pond, long shadows.

## 2. Glow Caverns

Under the hills: dark rock lit by mushrooms, crystals and pools of glowing goo. The first world where darkness is the default and light is something you go towards.

- **Teaches:** being runny. Warm springs and vents melt him; runny, he pours into cracks, squeezes under low rocks, hangs off a ledge on a thread to reach down, and drips if he goes too far. Cooling sets him again.
- **Puzzles:** a lost bit on a ledge below: dangle down on a thread, touch it, pull back; a crack too narrow for round Bob; drip a piece of yourself onto a switch below; carry heat through a cold passage before you set.
- **Later:** absorbing glow goo to become a light (world 6 brings absorbing; the caverns can hint at it).
- **Shot:** a runny Bob hangs from a mossy ledge on a long thread over a glowing pool, reaching for a lost bit on a ledge below; a drop has just fallen. Mushrooms, crystals, spores.

## 3. Sticky Ruins

A temple swallowed by jungle: carved blocks, roots, vines and walls running with tree resin, some patches of strange violet gum. Sun comes down in shafts through holes in the canopy.

- **Teaches:** climbing. Push into a tacky wall to hold, up to climb, down to climb down, let go to drop. Grip by colour: **moss** (green) is grippy ground, **resin** (amber) holds if you push in, **gum** (violet) holds on a sheer wall. Climbing sheds crumbs: a clean climb loses less.
- **Puzzles:** choose a route by colour; climb under an overhang; a resin wall in a sun shaft is softer and grips better (heat changes tack: an idea to test); drop from a wall onto a lower ledge.
- **Shot:** Bob, a drop of goo, halfway up a gum wall, crumbs falling; a resin route to one side glowing amber in a sun shaft; a lost bit on a carved ledge at the top.

## 4. Frozen Grotto

Snow, icicles, frozen waterfalls and black water under thin ice. Campfires are the only warmth.

- **Teaches:** cold. Chilled, he's stiff and slow; frozen solid, he can't roll or jump, slides on ice like a block, and cracks into chunks if he falls. Frozen, he **freezes water around him**: a frozen Bob can make his own bridge. Fires thaw him back.
- **Puzzles:** freeze yourself to cross a pool, thaw on the far side to jump; slide a frozen Bob down an ice chute to press a switch; get through a cold passage before you freeze; don't drop when frozen.
- **Shot:** moonlit lake. A frozen, faceted Bob has slid out onto the water and frozen a path behind him; on the far bank a campfire throws warm light onto the snow. Icicles, a frozen waterfall.

## 5. The Foundry

An abandoned works, still running: steel walkways, molten metal pours, steam leaks, pistons, grates, pressure plates and gates.

- **Teaches:** splitting on purpose. Cut a piece off (on a blade, or with a split action), leave it on a pressure plate to hold a gate, steer the rest through, then come back for it. Switching control between pieces. Machines that push, crush and carry.
- **Puzzles:** a plate that holds a gate open only while weighed down; a grate only runny Bob gets through, over a heat vent that makes him runny; spikes that slice him on purpose; reuniting before the exit to keep your score.
- **Shot:** a small piece of Bob sits on a glowing pressure plate, holding a gate open; the rest of him, runny from the vent's heat, pours through the gate towards the exit. Molten metal pours behind, steam, blue work lamps.

## 6. The Sump

The drains under the foundry and the bog they flood into: mud, oil slicks, tar, toxic gas, methane bubbling up, rotting wood.

- **Teaches:** absorbing. Water swells him (bigger, heavier, slower: enough to tip a see-saw); squeezing it out over a flame makes steam. Glow goo makes him a lamp that fades. Oil makes him slippery and flammable. Tar holds him; methane lights.
- **Puzzles:** swell to tip a balance; light a dark tunnel by carrying goo; burn off a tar plug with burning oil (but not while oily); time a run through gas between flares.
- **Shot:** a swollen, watery Bob weighs down one end of a log see-saw, lifting a lost bit up to a ledge; toxic gas glows green in the hollows, a methane bubble flares orange.

## 7. The Core

Down where it's hot. Lava rivers, obsidian, the remains of every world above, and a long climb out against the heat. The finale combines everything: melt to pour, freeze on lava-cooled stone, climb resin, split to hold plates, swell to cross. Not shot yet.

## Between worlds

- A world map that is also the side view of the earth: the Green on top, the caverns and ruins below, the grotto and foundry deeper, the sump under the foundry, the core at the bottom. Bob literally goes down.
- Each world ends with a gate weighed by mass: how much of Bob you bring decides how much opens (a bonus room, not a block).
- Bob's colour can follow gameplay without changing who he is: tinted by what he has absorbed, by temperature, or (later) by a world's special goo. His default colour is the anchor (see [02](02-art-direction.md#bobs-colour)).
