The game

A 2D platformer where everything is made of simulated pixels, including the hero. Bob is a blob of goo, a few hundred particles you steer by deforming them. Rock holds, sand pours, water flows, fire spreads, ice melts, and they change Bob: warm him and he goes runny, chill him and he goes stiff and brittle. Every puzzle is physics, and your body is the tool.

Everything is stuff

Every pixel is a material that can be dug, poured, burnt, frozen or pushed. No painted collision boxes.

Bob is stuff too

He squashes, flows, hangs on a thread, splits and pulls himself back together. How he feels to move matters more than anything.

Materials change Bob

The world is your toolkit and your threat. Get him into the right state: runny to pour through a crack, frozen to bridge water.

Readable chaos

The sim gets busy, but Bob and anything dangerous always read at a glance. Colours are a language you learn.

Why it's fun

  • Your body is the puzzle piece. No keys, no levers you carry: you are the thing that pours, plugs, hangs, weighs and freezes.
  • Mass is health and score. Spikes, lava and long drops cost you bits of Bob. Each world asks how much of you gets home, and the lost bits wait where they fell.
  • Many answers. Physics, not scripts: melt through, or climb round, or freeze across. Later worlds have three or four ways past every room.
  • Sloppy is forgiving. Bob is soft and slow to die. He sags off ledges, clings, oozes back. Built for thumbs on a phone as much as keys.
  • Skill shows. A clean climb sheds fewer crumbs; a gentle landing doesn't splash. Playing well keeps more of you.

Bob

Bob is goo: a viscoelastic fluid of about 280 particles, one per pixel of him. Pressure and surface tension keep him together, springs between particles stretch and flow, and a will to be round makes him a blob. Temperature turns those knobs continuously, so nothing about him ever switches. Every Bob on this page is the real one, simulated into his pose.

Heat only comes from things you can see: braziers, lava, vents, ice, snow. He soaks it up on the side facing the source, holds it for a while, and gives it off. Hot, he melts snow; frozen, he freezes the water he touches.

Look and feel

Crisp pixels, a lit simulated layer over a dim back wall, and colour that comes from light and from things that glow. Moody by default: most of every screen is dark, and the light tells you where to look.

320×180 camera

One world pixel is one simulated cell, scaled by whole numbers to the screen. Bob is about 20 px across: a sixteenth of the screen.

Light is real

Sky light and lamps cast soft shadows; light seeps a few pixels into rock; glowing things light their surroundings and bloom.

Two-colour light

Every world has a warm and a cool source fighting over the screen. The warm one is usually where the heat, the danger and the help are.

Colours are a language

Terrain is dark and low in saturation; saturation is kept for Bob, glow, hazards and anything you can use. A player who knows the colours can read a room before touching it.

Bob's colour

Worlds

A run of worlds, each a biome with its own materials and its own light, each teaching Bob one new thing, then mixing it with everything before. Bob literally goes down: from the meadows on the surface to the core of the earth.

The map

The world map is a cross-section of the earth. Each world is deeper than the last; the path between them is the route Bob took.

Menus and HUD

The game stays on screen behind every menu: menus are a dark glass panel and a pixel font, and they never take more than they need.
