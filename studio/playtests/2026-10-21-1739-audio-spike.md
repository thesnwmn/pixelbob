## What they said
On a phone, with every voice on: no crackles.

"I found a lot of the noises a bit harsh and not 'squishy' enough. I'd like a more organic sound. Roll is very continuous. Bounce is very clunky."

Asked afterwards:
- Which sound is the clunky bounce: **both**, the landing and the jump.
- What the roll should be instead: **all of** pulses as he turns over, much quieter, and only some ground heard. "Different surfaces should give different sounds to some extent (same for landing/jump)."
- Which voices to keep: **all eight**, reworked.
- Harsh, not squishy, means: **wet and muffled**: less hiss and high end, softer attacks, more low, damp squelch (like a hand in slime). Not rubbery or toy-like, and no clips needed.
- The sound as it stands: **2**.
- Next: **a tuning pass in the prototype, then the game** (recommended).

Added while the report was written: "Maybe a future spike presents a soundboard to play with. A few options to listen to and compare."

No difficulty or frustration: it's a sound spike, not a level.

## Wrong
Nothing broken. Everything here is taste, for the tuning pass.

## Liked, and would change
- Keep the approach: every voice synthesised from the sim's numbers, no clips. The spike's verdict is in: it runs clean on a phone and the producer keeps all eight voices, so the [audio spike](../work/audio-spike.md) is done.
- Squishier and more organic throughout: wet and muffled, less hiss and high end, softer attacks, more low, damp squelch.
- Roll: not a steady hiss. Pulses as his surface turns over onto the ground, far quieter, and heard on some ground more than others.
- Landing and jump: both clunky today (the sine thud and the rising blorp). Squelchier, and coloured by the ground like the roll.
- Different ground should sound different, for rolling, landing and jumping alike.
- A soundboard: a few options per voice, to listen to and compare, rather than one take to judge.
- All of it goes to [audio-tune](../work/audio-tune.md): a tuning pass in `prototypes/04-audio/` for another listen, then the Engineer builds sound into the game.
