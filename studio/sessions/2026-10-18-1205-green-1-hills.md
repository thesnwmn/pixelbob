## Summary
`/go` picked green-1-hills: nothing needed the producer, no bugs or open PRs, and the producer asked for one level end to end first. Its needs had landed, so it went straight from shaped to doing. The first Green level is painted, with two checkpoints, a lost bit, a scenario with a handoff card and a kept replay that finishes it.

## Tried and dropped
- A 61-cell ravine and steps of 24, 28 and 34: a tap got up the 24, a standing full charge crossed the 61, and the 34 (and 28 to 30 for the second step) failed a good jump too often. Settled on 28 and 26 for the steps and 56 for the ravine, by measuring each from 8 nearby starts.
- The spike's search bot (with the levels loaded, not committed) stalled at x 906 after the ravine: its score only rewards going right. The kept run is a scripted driver instead: roll right, a full charge (22 frames) at 405, 772 and 1080, and a tap when he hasn't moved for 40 frames.

## The Tester
Played it headless with bots. No softlocks: Bob gets out of the ravine (collecting the bit) and over again, both checkpoints light and respawn him on the ground, and the edges hold him. #35 didn't bite from any start on this level. Found:
- From against its wall, a 4-frame tap got up the first step at 26 cells (20 of 20). Raised to 28: a tap fails 12 of 12, a full charge works 12 of 12. The second step, at 26 from a dip, already needed 16 frames or more.
- Tapping jump in a rhythm (2 frames in every 10 to 45) finishes the level with no charged jump, faster than the kept run. That's how Bob's taps work, not the level, so it's a question on the card rather than a change.
- A 6-frame hold late at the lip can clear the ravine: borderline.
- About 7% of Bob is shed along the way, mostly on landings, and `bob` (alive) still reads 100. About Bob in general, not this level.

## Learned
- What Bob can do on grass, for designing levels: he rolls up 0.75 slopes from rest (45° is chancy); a tap jumps about 15 cells high, a full charge about 30 and, from a standstill, across gaps of 80 or more; walls of 26 to 28 are sure with a full charge (but a tap from against a 26 can get up it), 30 and up are chancy. Rolling tops out near 85 px/s even downhill, so a run-up barely matters.
- The level was drawn from a height profile (knots joined by straight or eased runs, vertical steps) by a throwaway script, then checked with `node game/scripts/levels.ts`. A level tool that does this could save the Designer painting by hand; not needed yet.
