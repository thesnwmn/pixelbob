## Summary
`/go` picked the audio spike: nothing needed the producer but the green-1-hills playtest, the one bug (#36) is theirs for later, and their last playtest asked for sound to carry the opening. Question: can Bob's sound be synthesised live from the sim's numbers, cheaply, and does it sound like him? The cost half is answered yes; the sound half waits on the producer's ears.

## Done
- `prototypes/04-audio/` (`pnpm dev:audio`): the Bob playground plus sound.
  - `listen.ts` (no DOM) reads Bob after each step into continuous cues: roll (by speed, contact and ground: soft, hard, slick, sticky or sharp), goo wobble (particle motion against the piece once its spin is taken out), charge, wetness and sizzle (hot particles, bits boiled off). It also reads events: landings (touchdown after 6 frames in the air, as hard as he was falling), jumps (the frame his stretch is set, with the banked charge), splashes (once going in, then none until he's been out 15 frames) and cuts (impact breaks).
  - `synth.ts`: Web Audio, no clips. Noise beds through filters for roll, goo, water and sizzle; a low throb under the wobble; a squeeze tone while charging; shaped bursts for splats, boings, splashes, bubbles (rising sine chirps) and rips. A compressor goes on the master. Every voice can be toggled on the page.
  - `tour.ts` and `render.ts`: a scripted tour of the course, rendered offline in headless Chromium to a WAV with its levels per leg; `cues.ts` prints what a run hears, section by section.

## Tried and dropped
- Wobble from particle speeds against the piece's: rolling spins them round his middle, so it read 0.8 all the time. Taking out the rigid spin left 15 to 24 px/s of real jiggle as the baseline.
- Tears from strain breaks: his goo re-links all the time as he rolls (58 a section), so only impact breaks count.
- Landings from a change of speed in one frame: a landing spreads over 3 or 4 frames (46 to 61 px/s each), so it's touchdown after airtime instead.
- A splash on one frame's rise in wetness: rolling into water gently never made one.

## The Critic

Also taken: sounds no longer queue up while the sound is off, the folder joined the determinism scan, a `pnpm dev:audio` line in the docs, unused fields gone, and the verdict names what Bob would need to expose. The diff is about 980 lines, but most of it is the playground's `main.ts` copied in, with about 40 lines of sound wiring.

## Learned
- Bob already exposes most of what sound needs: per-particle contact material, the charge, the banked charge and the stretch, wetness per piece, temperatures, and losses and breaks counters.
- Reading the cues takes about 20 µs a frame in Node, and 95 µs in a slow headless browser with the synth's scheduling included. The synthesis itself is on the audio thread: offline on a desktop it runs 49 times faster than real time. Nothing here measured a phone.
- The first mix was a hiss: wide noise bands for the roll, and a sizzle that drowned everything else. Narrower bands and a much quieter sizzle balanced it (the legs sit between -24 and -42 dB rms, peaks -6 to -15, nothing clipped). I couldn't listen, so these were judged from a spectrogram and levels.
