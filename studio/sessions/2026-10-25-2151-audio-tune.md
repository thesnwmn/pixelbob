## Summary
`/go` with no arguments. Health and main were green. Nothing new waited on the producer beyond the gated PR #46 and the green-1-hills playtest, and bug #36 is "for later". So the pick was among the three ready units, and **audio-tune** went first: it acts on the producer's own rating (sound 2, "harsh, not squishy"), and whether synthesis alone can sound organic is the riskiest open question for the game's atmosphere. release-builds and music-spike are still ready.

## Done
- `prototypes/04-audio/`, reworked to sound wet and muffled ([the unit's log](../work/audio-tune.md#log) has the numbers):
  - A low-pass over everything (*muffle*), softer attacks, and squelches (noise through a resonant low-pass that sweeps and flutters) in place of sine thuds, blorps and sawtooth rips.
  - The roll comes in pulses from contacts made and lost (`touch`, `peel` in `listen.ts`), well back in the mix, and some ground is heard more than others.
  - Landing and jump are coloured by the ground: brightness, wetness, loudness. Resin and gum add a sticky tail.
  - A soundboard (the *Soundboard* button, or `?board`): three takes each for roll, landing and jump, played on a chosen ground, live while Bob moves, saved in the link (`roll-take`, `land-take`, `jump-take`, `muffle`).
- `render.ts` picks takes, the muffle and a solo voice, counts the roll's pulses, measures the loudness above 1.5 kHz, and with `board` renders every take on every ground.

## Tried and dropped
- Measuring brightness through one one-pole high-pass. The low thuds leaked through it, so cutting the hiss barely showed. Four poles show it.
- A roll pulse every 14 contacts. That's 12 to 15 a second, and 60 to 90 ms pats overlap into the continuous texture the producer disliked (the Critic caught it). It's now 40, which gives 3 to 5 a second, and the board's preview plays at the same rate.

## Learned
- The old mix's loudest voice was the goo's low sine throb, peaking at -7 dB on every landing: probably the "clunky" bounce as much as the landing voice was.
- Rolling at speed makes about 2 contacts and 2 losses a frame, on any ground. Ice differs only in how much he slides, so the synth hears it less often by ground, not from the contacts.
