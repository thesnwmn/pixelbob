## Done
- `prototypes/05-music/`: green-1-hills from the game with a generative score and Bob's voices from the audio spike on one audio context ([the unit's log](../work/music-spike.md#log) has the verdict and the numbers).
  - `follow.ts` reads the game into eight eased numbers (energy, height, air, wind, wet, warmth, progress, rest); `score.ts` writes notes from them a sixteenth at a time, seeded; `band.ts` plays them (pad, bass, melody, arpeggio, bell, an echo and a hall), dips where Bob's squelches are and ducks when he lands or jumps.
  - The page: *Listen* plays a kept run, *Mix* sets the levels and turns parts off (saved in the link), and the HUD shows the mood and what the music costs a frame.
  - `pnpm notes` prints what the music does over a kept run in Node; `render.ts` renders it offline in Chromium and measures cost and levels.
- The determinism check covers the new prototype; `pnpm dev:music`; the prototypes in `docs/tech-and-toolchain.md`.

## Tried and dropped
- A three-second hall: the band rendered 11 times faster than real time, against 35 for Bob's voices. Two seconds gets 15.
- The music at Bob's volume: it was louder than him overall and where his squelches are. It's 0.3 now, with a deeper dip at 350 Hz.

## Learned
- The kept run hardly charges (the charge peaks for a third of a second), so the wind-up arpeggio is a flourish of two to four notes. A level with long wind-ups would hear it more.
- The prototype imports the game's level, `play.ts` and replays straight from `game/`, which made "play it over green-1-hills" cheap. Spikes about the game can do that rather than copy the playground.
